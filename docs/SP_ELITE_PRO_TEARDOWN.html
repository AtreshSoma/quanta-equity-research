What changed

All forty-eight findings are fixed. The engineering discipline was never the problem — lint was clean and 470 tests passed throughout. The problems were semantic: the code did precisely what it said, and what it said was wrong. The suite now runs 531 tests, including 74 named for the findings they lock in, so each of these fails loudly if it comes back.

Three fixes account for most of the difference. A “standard” scan now ranks the whole 669-name universe on liquidity instead of taking the alphabetical first 100 — all twelve mega-caps are reachable where none were, and full covers every name. Position sizes are derived from the stop, so dollar risk at the stop is the risk budget, and sizes differentiate by conviction and volatility: 7 distinct sizes across a 9-case grid where there was 1. And the trailing stop no longer publishes a level 0.5% below entry — ratcheting now requires a real high-since-entry, which a scanner does not have for a new position, so it stops inventing one.

The one behaviour change you will notice immediately: far fewer names clear the gates. On a 24-name mega-cap scan, 2 passed where nearly all would have before. That is the intended effect — the engine no longer manufactures the reward side of a trade, so risk_reward < 1.8 can finally reject something. Observed R:R now spans 0.30 to 3.4 across real names instead of being satisfied by construction. A thin day producing two ideas is the honest answer, not a bug.

The ledger

Forty-eight findings in four severity bands. Each carries what was wrong, why it mattered at the desk, and what the fix does. All are resolved in commit e8e01df.

Show

Blockers

7 findings

Each of these on its own was enough to make the printed order sheet unsafe to act on. All seven are fixed.

B1

The scan universe is an alphabetical slice

build_us_stock_universe · reproduced

What
The builder assembles 669 names, sorts them, then does final_universe = universe_list[:limit]. standard takes the first 100 (A→BSY); full the first 200 (A→~G). _even_sample() exists for exactly this purpose and is never called on this path.
Why
The engine cannot see roughly 85% of its own universe, and which 15% it sees is decided by the alphabet. Every “top pick” is the best of an arbitrary sub-sample. This alone voids any comparison between runs, any backtest, and any claim of breadth.
Fixed
Rank the universe on a cheap, real criterion (dollar volume, or the existing prefilter score) and take the top N. Never slice a sorted-by-symbol list. Assert in a test that the selected set is not a lexicographic prefix.
B2

The published trailing stop sits 0.5% below entry

update_trailing_stops · reproduced

What
It uses high_52w as “recent high since entry”, subtracts 1.75 ATR, then clamps with min(candidate, current_price * 0.995). For any name whose 52-week high is more than ~1.75 ATR above spot — which is most of them — the clamp always wins.
Why
The value is written to Trailing_Stop in the CSV and printed in the markdown report. Acting on it stops you out the same session, and it silently raises the effective stop above the hard stop the analysis actually derived.
Fixed
A trailing stop needs a running high since entry, which requires position state this module does not have. Until it does, do not emit the field: publish the initial ATR stop and a documented ratchet rule instead of a number that looks executable.
B3

Every risk-based position size is computed, then discarded

_calculate_position_size → get_top_recommendations · reproduced

What
_calculate_position_size layers volatility targeting, a signal multiplier, a score multiplier, an ATR haircut and the drawdown throttle; the risk-grade and FOMC multipliers are applied on top. Then the allocator overwrites all of it with position_size = min(available_capital / max_positions, remaining, cap, sector_room). analysis.allocation appears only on the left-hand side.
Why
Sizing is equal-weight. A 5-star, 18%-vol, grade-A name and a 2-star, 45%-vol, grade-C name get identical dollars. All that risk machinery survives only as a pass / fail gate, which is also why the volatility targeting is inert: 0.04 / vol exceeds the 10% cap for any name under ~27% vol, so nearly everything pins to the cap anyway.
Fixed
Pick one sizing authority. Either honour analysis.allocation in the allocator and use target_per_position only as a ceiling, or delete _calculate_position_size and be explicit that the book is equal-weight. Two sizers where one silently wins is the worst of both.
B4

There is no per-trade risk cap anywhere

TradingConfig.MAX_RISK_PER_TRADE · reproduced

What
MAX_RISK_PER_TRADE (2%) and MAX_RISK_STRONG_BUY (2.5%) are referenced in exactly one place: validate_config, which checks they are in range. Nothing ever ties share count to entry - stop. MAX_DRAWDOWN_THRESHOLD and RISK_REWARD_OPTIMAL are likewise unused.
Why
Position risk is whatever falls out of equal weight times whatever stop distance the target function happened to produce. The summary panel's “Max Risk” line is descriptive only — no gate reads it. A wide volatility stop on a capped position can quietly exceed the stated budget with nothing to catch it.
Fixed
Size from risk: shares = (capital × risk_pct) / (entry - stop), then clamp by the position cap, sector room and liquidity. Add a portfolio-level gate on Σ shares × (entry - stop) against MAX_PORTFOLIO_RISK, which currently only governs VaR.
B5

The optimizer maximizes on six months of trailing returns

get_top_recommendations, mean-variance block

What
mu = returns_df.mean().values * 252 — historical sample means as expected returns, on roughly 126 observations, for an N×N covariance. It then overrides the equal weights and forces them to sum to 1.0.
Why
Textbook error maximization: Markowitz on sample means concentrates into whatever ran hardest, with estimation error swamping signal at this sample size. Worse, mu is completely disconnected from the engine's own alpha score — final position sizes are set by six months of trailing returns, not by the model. Forcing weights to sum to 1.0 also destroys the honest under-deployment signal the code deliberately preserved by deleting its old fill passes.
Fixed
Drop the return forecast. Use minimum-variance or inverse-volatility weights (no mu), or feed the composite score in as the expected-return proxy with heavy shrinkage and a Ledoit–Wolf covariance. Keep the sum-of-weights constraint ≤ 1, not = 1.
B6

Risk/reward is inflated on both sides by construction

_calculate_price_targets · reproduced

What
The stop is max() of three candidates — which selects the tightest, so the volatility and support stops almost never bind. The target is max() of ATR, Fibonacci and volatility candidates, then blended 75% to the sell-side mean target — and only when that target is above spot, so bearish analyst views are silently discarded. When R:R still falls short, the target is simply pushed out to entry + risk × 1.8.
Why
Smallest plausible denominator against the largest plausible numerator. The R:R ≥ 1.8 gate cannot bind on anything real, and the reward side of every trade is effectively a sell-side price-target screen. Using annualized volatility as a price target is also dimensionally wrong — a 40%-vol stock gets a +40% target because it is volatile.
Fixed
Choose stop and target from one coherent method (ATR multiple, or structure) rather than an extremum across methods. Use analyst targets symmetrically or not at all. Never manufacture a target to satisfy the R:R gate — let the name fail the gate.
B7

“Expected value” is positive by construction and rises with risk

_calculate_price_scenarios, PriceScenarios · reproduced

What
The bull leg is price × (1 + vol × 1.5), the bear leg price × (1 - vol × 0.75) — half the sigma multiple. Ordering clamps then floor base at 1.05× and bull at 1.155×. Probability weights are hardcoded 25 / 50 / 25 with no basis.
Why
EV is mechanically positive for every stock and increases with volatility: +5.8% at 15% vol, +12.4% at 50%. A field named “expected value” that rewards risk and can never be negative is worse than no field. It reaches the JSON export via asdict.
Fixed
Make the sigma multiples symmetric and drop the ordering floors, or replace the whole thing with an empirical forward-return distribution. If the weights are not estimated, do not publish an EV.

Wrong numbers

13 findings

The code executes without error and produces an incorrect value.

W1

detect_regime can never return BULL or BEAR

MarketRegimeDetector.detect_regime · reproduced

What
Called with spy_hist=None, so it fetches period="6mo" (~126 bars). Then sma_200 = rolling(200) if len ≥ 200 else sma_50, making the test price > sma_50 > sma_200 evaluate sma_50 > sma_50 — always false. Same for the bear branch.
Why
The engine is permanently stuck in RANGING or VOLATILE. The momentum-tilted BULL weights and value-tilted BEAR weights never apply. Cash guidance is pinned at 35% instead of 15%. RANGING's 8.5 excellent-threshold always binds. Confirmation #2 of the confidence tier is permanently degraded, and BULL's wider 2.5× target safety cap is never reachable.
Fixed
Fetch period="2y" for regime detection, and when history is short, mark the trend regime UNKNOWN rather than substituting sma_50 for sma_200. A dead branch that silently changes portfolio policy is the most expensive kind.
W2

Realized volatility substituted for implied

detect_regime, VIX fallback chain

What
When ^VIX fails, the fallback computes 20-day realized annualized volatility and assigns it to vix_value.
Why
Realized sits below implied most of the time (the variance risk premium). The fallback therefore biases the regime toward LOW volatility, where recommended_exposure is 1.2× — it levers up precisely when the real signal is missing.
Fixed
If VIX is unavailable, hold the last-good value (the cache already exists) or degrade to NORMAL. Never let a proxy justify above-1.0 exposure. If you keep the proxy, scale it by a documented premium and label it as a proxy in the header.
W3

ADX violates Wilder's directional-movement rule

TechnicalAnalysisEngine._calculate_adx · reproduced

What
plus_dm[plus_dm < 0] = 0; minus_dm[minus_dm < 0] = 0 — the negatives are zeroed but the mutual-exclusivity test is missing. Wilder requires +DM = upMove if upMove > downMove else 0. On an outside bar the code keeps both.
Why
+DI and −DI are both overstated, so DX and ADX are wrong on every outside bar. ADX feeds technical_score (1.0 pt), momentum_score (2.0 pts) and trend_strength — it is one of the most heavily weighted single inputs in the model.
Fixed
Add the exclusivity test, and test against a known-good ADX series (ta-lib or a hand-computed fixture) rather than only asserting the output is finite.
W4

Two different values for the same 12–2 momentum factor

TechnicalAnalysisEngine vs EliteQuantEngine · reproduced

What
One uses iloc[-44]/iloc[-252], the other iloc[-42]/iloc[-252]. The inline comment claims “skip last 22 trading days” while the code skips 42–44. momentum_12m is aliased to momentum_12_2, so a field labelled 12-month return is not one.
Why
The same named factor takes two values in the same run, and the documented definition matches neither. Any attribution or factor-loading work built on this is unreconcilable.
Fixed
One implementation, one constant for the skip window, imported by both call sites. Remove the momentum_12m alias or compute it honestly.
W5

Debt/equity normalized two incompatible ways

analyze_fundamentals vs calculate_qmj_score · reproduced

What
The fundamental engine always divides by 100 (correct — Yahoo reports a percentage). QMJ uses raw/100 if raw > 10 else raw. A reported 5.0 means 0.05×; QMJ reads it as 5.0×.
Why
A company with almost no debt is assigned junk_score = 3.0 and a negative QMJ spread. The same conditional-conversion bug is present for dividendYield (> 1.0 heuristic) while the other path uses normalize_dividend_yield. Yahoo has already changed the yield format once.
Fixed
Normalize once, at ingestion. Every downstream consumer reads FundamentalMetrics, never raw info. Conditional unit conversion is always a latent bug — the condition eventually matches a legitimate value.
W6

The decision core upgrades past the scanner's own gates

analyze_stock, FinalAction block · reproduced

What
analysis.signal is overwritten unconditionally by _fa_to_signal[final_action], and _candidate_from_score is score-only. A 7.6 score maps to STRONG_BUY regardless of MACD, volume, momentum or Sharpe.
Why
The comment says the core exists so the scanner “can never publish a recommendation more bullish than the deep analyzer” — but this path is bidirectional. It promotes names past the four technical confirmations _determine_signal_regime_aware deliberately requires for STRONG_BUY. Three separate threshold tables must also be hand-synced: TradingConfig (5.0 / 6.0 / 7.5), the regime table (6.0 / 8.5), and the engine (5.5 / 6.5 / 7.5). The R:R floors disagree too — 1.5 in the engine, 1.8 in config.
Fixed
Apply the core's verdict only when it is more conservative — the same _ACTION_RANK comparison already used in deep-confirm. Move the technical confirmations into DecisionInputs so the core can see them, and collapse the three threshold tables into one.
W7

Drawdown is measured from cost basis, not peak equity

_estimate_current_drawdown

What
Returns max(0, -(total_current / total_entry - 1)) — unrealized P&L against cost, not decline from a high-water mark. Cash is ignored. It also makes one or two sequential yfinance calls per holding.
Why
A book up 25% and then back to +5% reports 0% drawdown after giving back a fifth of its gains. The 5% / 10% / 15% throttle — including the hard trading halt — therefore fires on the wrong quantity, and in practice almost never fires.
Fixed
Persist a daily equity curve and compute 1 - equity / running_max. Until that exists, the throttle should be disabled rather than reading a number that means something else.
W8

save_portfolio overwrites holdings with recommendations

save_portfolio / load_portfolio

What
It writes today's suggestions to portfolio.csv with entry_date = today and scan-time prices. Both check_downgrades and _estimate_current_drawdown read that same file.
Why
The “portfolio” becomes whatever was last suggested, not what was bought. That is the deeper reason drawdown reads ~0 forever, and why downgrade detection compares against the previous scan rather than against real entries. The state file is self-corrupting on every run.
Fixed
Separate positions.csv (actual fills, append-only, never written by a scan) from signals.csv (scan output). Reconcile the two explicitly. A scan should never be able to mutate the position ledger.
W9

check_downgrades never sees a REDUCE

check_downgrades · reproduced

What
The priority map keys are STRONG_BUY BUY ACCUMULATE HOLD SELL AVOID, but SignalType emits REDUCE, not SELL. Unmapped values fall back to 2 — the same priority as HOLD.
Why
A HOLD → REDUCE deterioration raises no alert. Since exits are where most of the money is lost, a silent gap on the sell side is the expensive direction to be wrong in.
Fixed
Derive the priority map from SignalType.priority, which already exists, instead of restating the strings. Fail loudly on an unmapped signal rather than defaulting to neutral.
W10

The score keeps changing after the signal is derived

analyze_stock

What
signal is set from overall_score, then the score absorbs a chase penalty (−1.5), a contrarian penalty (−1.0), a consensus bonus (+0.3), a catalyst boost (up to +2.0), an insider adjustment (±0.8) and a revision adjustment — with only a single STRONG_BUY → BUY trim in response.
Why
The published score and the published action are derived from different numbers and can openly disagree. A headline-NLP catalyst boost is worth up to 20% of the entire 0–10 scale — the largest single lever in the model belongs to its least reliable input, and it is applied after the minimum-score gate.
Fixed
Compute all adjustments first, then derive the signal once from the final score. Cap the total adjustment budget (±1.0 is generous) and log each component so the composite is auditable.
W11

position_pct is stale whenever the optimizer is skipped

get_top_recommendations, greedy pass · reproduced

What
The greedy pass sets analysis.shares and analysis.allocation but never refreshes analysis.position_pct. Only the mean-variance branch updates it.
Why
With fewer than two picks, no scipy, or a non-converging solve, the card and the rationale print a dollar figure and a “% of portfolio” that do not correspond to each other — the percentage still reflects the discarded volatility-target sizing from B3.
Fixed
Make position_pct a derived property of allocation / capital rather than a stored field. Any denormalized field that two code paths must remember to update will eventually not be updated.
W12

smart_prefilter is unreachable for every built-in scan

scan_market

What
The gate is len(universe) > 200 and scan_type in ['full','comprehensive','standard','large_cap']. Those scan types cap at 200, 150, 100 and 120 respectively, so the condition is never true.
Why
“PHASE 1: SMART PRE-SCREENING” never runs. Combined with B1, the intended design — rank the whole universe, deep-scan the best — is entirely inoperative, replaced by an alphabetical prefix.
Fixed
Run the prefilter on the full universe before applying the scan limit, and make the limit the prefilter's max_candidates. That resolves B1 and W12 with the same change.
W13

The entire elite-factor layer is dead weight

EliteQuantEngine, EliteFactorScores · reproduced

What
elite_composite_score is computed and never read. So are check_factor_vif, calculate_true_factor_loadings, MarketRegime.factor_weights (a second, unused regime-weight table with a growth key the scorer does not know), UniverseStats._percentile_rank, and _even_sample on the main path.
Why
QMJ, betting-against-beta, the style factors and 12–2 momentum are computed, displayed and exported — and influence neither ranking nor sizing. The most impressive-looking part of the engine is decorative, which makes the output read as more quantitatively grounded than it is.
Fixed
Either wire elite_composite_score into the composite with a stated weight and validate it, or delete the layer. Call check_factor_vif — it exists precisely for M1.

Modelling

14 findings

Method-level problems. These are why the README's “no demonstrated out-of-sample edge” is the honest position.

M1

Factors are double-counted across sub-scores

ScoringEngine · reproduced

What
ADX appears in both technical_score and momentum_score — 0.35 combined weight. P/E, PEG and ROE appear in both fundamental_score and value_score — 0.45 combined. Within momentum, momentum_1m (20d) and roc (13d) are near-duplicates. Price-versus-moving-average appears in technical while the same trend information drives momentum.
Why
Nominal weights are not effective weights. The composite is far more of a trend bet than the 15 / 20 technical-plus-momentum split suggests, and valuation gets counted twice.
Fixed
Assign each metric to exactly one sub-score. Run check_factor_vif on the sub-scores and report the correlation matrix — if two sub-scores correlate above ~0.8, they are one factor.
M2

The sub-scores contradict each other

ScoringEngine, EliteQuantEngine, smart_prefilter

What
technical_score pays +1.7 for oversold RSI and +1.0 for strong ADX trend. quality_score rewards beta near 1.0 while bab_score rewards low beta. quality_score gives mega-caps +2.0 while size_factor gives them −1.5. And smart_prefilter drops anything more than 25% off its 6-month high — killing the “RSI oversold paradox” STRONG_BUY path in the same file.
Why
A score that rewards a condition and its opposite is not measuring anything. It cannot be interpreted, attributed or improved, and it compresses toward the middle so cross-sectional ranking degrades.
Fixed
Pick a thesis per sub-score and let it be directional. If you want both trend-following and mean-reversion, run them as two separate strategies with separate sleeves — not as offsetting terms inside one number.
M3

Relative scores judged against absolute thresholds

normalize_scores → gates

What
normalize_scores converts each sub-score to a percentile of the scanned set via norm.cdf, blended 70 / 30 with the heuristic. Those now-relative scores are then compared against fixed cut-offs (5.0 / 6.0 / 7.5, and 55 / 65 / 75 in the core).
Why
A percentile has no absolute meaning: the best decile of a falling market scores the same as the best decile of a rising one. The z-score population is also the survivor set — names that already passed every gate — so it is not the market's distribution. And because five correlated percentile scores are averaged, the composite concentrates near 5–6.5, which is why the 7.5 STRONG_BUY threshold rarely fires and the median gate ends up binding instead.
Fixed
Decide which the score is. If relative, replace absolute gates with rank cut-offs and add an absolute quality veto. If absolute, z-score against a fixed reference universe snapshot, not against the survivors.
M4

Quality score is 40% size and dividend, 0% quality

calculate_quality_score

What
Of 10 points: Sharpe 3.0, max drawdown 2.0, market cap 2.0, beta 1.0, dividend yield 2.0. No ROIC, no gross-margin stability, no accruals, no interest coverage, no earnings variability.
Why
This is a low-volatility-plus-large-cap-plus-payout factor wearing a quality label. It structurally penalizes every non-dividend compounder, and its size tilt directly opposes the SMB factor computed elsewhere (M2). Two of the five inputs are past price behaviour, not business quality.
Fixed
Rebuild on balance-sheet and income-statement quality — gross profitability, ROIC, accruals, leverage, earnings stability. Move size and payout to their own factors where their sign can be argued explicitly.
M5

Three fundamentals read keys yfinance does not return

analyze_fundamentals

What

M6

Time-to-target uses X / σ where it needs (X / σ)²

What
base_months = abs(expected_return) / monthly_volatility. For a diffusion, expected time to travel distance X at per-period volatility σ scales with the square of that ratio.
Why
Timelines are far too fast, and increasingly so for larger moves: a 3-sigma target is projected at 3 months when the same assumption implies about 9. Every “3–6 months” label in the output inherits the error.
Fixed
Use (X/σ)², or drop the projection to a volatility-scaled horizon band without implying a hitting time. The sector multipliers layered on top are unvalidated and add false precision either way.

Jensen's alpha omits the risk-free rate

What

Why

Subtract the risk-free rate from both legs, using the same constant as calculate_risk_metrics. Consider geometric annualization for Sharpe.

1.8 ATR stops against multi-month targets

What
Because the stop is max() of three candidates (B6), the ATR stop nearly always wins: roughly 4–5% below entry for a typical name. Targets are framed at 3–6 months.
Why

What
